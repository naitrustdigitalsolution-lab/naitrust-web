Yes—this makes the model stronger. The agent should create a trusted release checkpoint, while the buyer remains the final payment authority.
Addition to the plan
Agent-certified supplier payments
The sourcing agent’s button should be:
Certify products & request supplier payment
It should not directly move money. The flow becomes:
Customer funds the order through a partner-issued collection account.
Product funds remain assigned to that specific supplier order.
The sourcing agent completes the required checks.
The agent uploads evidence and signs a readiness declaration.
The buyer reviews the evidence.
The buyer approves full or partial supplier payment.
The licensed payment partner pays the verified supplier.
Every fee, conversion and settlement receipt appears in the Order Room.
The agent can certify:
Supplier identity and operating location checked.
Product specifications matched.
Quantity confirmed.
Customization or sample approved.
Production completed.
Final inspection passed.
Products placed in agent or verified warehouse custody.
Products handed to the logistics provider.
The app should call this a readiness certification, not a guarantee. “Guarantee” could imply that the agent or Naitrust accepts financial liability.
Part-payment milestones
Custom manufacturing frequently requires a deposit. Each quote should therefore support:
One payment after final inspection.
Deposit and balance.
Multiple production milestones.
Example:
20% after supplier verification and sample approval.
30% after production evidence.
50% after final inspection and confirmed custody.
For every milestone:
Agent selects “Recommend release.”
Required evidence must be complete.
Buyer selects “Approve payment.”
Transaction PIN confirms approval.
Payment partner performs settlement.
If the buyer requests changes or disputes the evidence, the milestone remains unpaid. There should be no automatic payment based only on the agent’s recommendation.
For high-value or high-risk orders, Naitrust can require admin review, an independent second inspector, or warehouse confirmation before enabling buyer approval.
